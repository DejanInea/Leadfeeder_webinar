01 / Executive summary

From identifying visitors to understanding marketing impact

Leadfeeder presented Web Insights as a new way to evaluate the quality of website traffic, and an upcoming AI Assistant to make work across the platform easier to execute.

The central question was whether channels, campaigns and pages attract the companies a business actually wants—not simply whether they generate visits. Web Insights adds company and ideal customer profile (ICP) context to analytics. The AI Assistant is intended to perform tasks across Leadfeeder through natural-language requests.

Main takeaway

Use company fit, engagement and goal conversions together to judge marketing performance. Then use lists and workflows to turn qualified website activity into research, targeting and CRM actions.

Metrics to read together

  • Traffic volume: sessions, visits, page views and unique visitors show activity, but do not establish customer fit.
  • Company fit: identified companies, ICP ratio and segment distribution help assess audience relevance. The exact ICP-ratio denominator was not defined in the webinar.
  • Engagement: intent and relevant page activity help distinguish meaningful interest from general traffic.
  • Goal conversions: configured goals represent important website actions and let teams compare sources and campaigns on those actions.
  • Spend: advertising integrations add budget context. Spend, traffic and website goals alone do not establish revenue attribution.

Immediate capability versus longer-term ambition

The broader direction includes understanding pipeline and revenue impact. The first version demonstrated focused on traffic, ICP/company analysis, pages, campaigns and website goals. A complete pipeline/revenue attribution model was not demonstrated or specified. 24:11 → 27:34 →

04 / AI Assistant

Two distinct uses of AI

Ask questions inside Web Insights

The demonstrated feature answers analytical questions about website data. The example “How are my campaigns performing?” produced a visual answer with metrics and a table.

Purpose: explore and explain the data.

Leadfeeder Agent across the platform

The planned assistant goes beyond reports to perform work such as building workflows and enriching lists. It was described as an alternative to manually navigating multiple pages.

Purpose: execute tasks and reach a requested outcome.

The agent was still being built. The presenter suggested availability later in the year of the recording and said beta participants could receive access when it was ready. The webinar did not establish agent pricing, usage limits, permissions, approval behavior or exact task coverage. 44:03 →

05 / Access & limitations

What to know before planning adoption

  • Web Insights was not yet launched. Attendees were invited to join the beta through a webinar poll. The team would follow up and notify participants by email. That poll is a recording-era enrollment route, not a confirmed current sign-up method.
  • Existing-plan inclusion: speakers said Web Insights would be included in existing Leadfeeder plans, with a free version also available. Specific free-tier limits were not given.
  • Tracking: existing customers with the script installed were told no new script was needed. ICP definitions and goal configuration were emphasized to get useful results.
  • Historical data: existing customers were promised data for the current year of the recording. A general retention policy or guaranteed history for new accounts was not specified.
  • Ads: Google Ads and LinkedIn Ads analysis depends on the relevant integrations being set up.
  • API/MCP: Web Insights reports were not yet exposed through these interfaces. The stated sequence was UI first, customer feedback and improvements, then API/MCP rollout.
  • Gemini: it was not officially supported for MCP at the time. Claude, ChatGPT, Cursor and Copilot were named as supported interfaces.

06 / Audience Q&A

Operational questions answered

How are paid and organic sources distinguished?

Leadfeeder uses source and medium information, including UTM tags. The presenter described automatic tagging where possible, with organic traffic using an organic medium and paid traffic defaulting to CPC. Custom UTM-medium values can change those labels. A free UTM builder on Leadfeeder’s website was also mentioned. 50:09 →

How do we send new ICP-fit website companies to HubSpot?

Use Web Visitors to identify company visits and custom feeds to define relevant activity. Define ICP segments so matching companies are tagged. Connect the feeds to workflows that can research companies, find contacts and send records to HubSpot on a configured schedule. 52:08 →

What does “signals” mean?

The response described relevant company events: expansion, competitive context, customer acquisition news, mergers and acquisitions, hiring, leadership changes, and partnerships. AI enrichment can research these signals, which can then inform workflow actions. This answer concerns company events; the intent-score discussion separately described website engagement signals. 55:08 →

07 / Recommended actions

A practical implementation checklist

Suggested next steps derived from the webinar; these are not commitments made by Leadfeeder.

  1. Confirm access and commercial terms. Ask the account team about current Web Insights and agent availability, enrollment, plan coverage, limits and retention.
  2. Check tracking and integrations. Confirm the tracking script is working on the intended sites and connect the advertising and CRM systems needed for the analysis.
  3. Define customer fit. Agree ICP segments using criteria such as industry, company size and geography. Review which companies match before relying on the ICP ratio.
  4. Define measurable website goals. Choose actions that matter to the business and verify they are being recorded. Standardize UTM source, medium and campaign naming.
  5. Establish a baseline. Compare channels and campaigns using volume, company fit, engagement, goal conversions and spend where available. Examine trends and segment mix before changing budgets.
  6. Pilot one qualification workflow. Start with a clearly defined feed, such as ICP-fit companies visiting relevant product or pricing pages. Add research and contact enrichment, then verify CRM field mapping and routing.
  7. Review outcomes and refine. Check the relevance of leads received, conversion outcomes and audience updates. Improve the qualification rules and report useful beta feedback.

Questions still worth confirming

  • How exactly is ICP ratio calculated, and how is unidentified traffic handled in each report?
  • What are the refresh intervals, data retention rules, free-tier limits and historical-data conditions?
  • Which attribution models, pipeline/revenue metrics and integrations are supported in the available version?
  • When will Web Insights API/MCP endpoints and the broader agent become available?
  • What controls govern agent actions, permissions, approvals, audit history and usage costs?

08 / Reference

Glossary & follow-up resources

ICP
Ideal customer profile: criteria describing companies that are a good fit for the business.
Intent
Signals of interest; in the scoring feature, recent website engagement, frequency and visitor breadth.
CRM
Customer relationship management system, such as HubSpot.
UTM
URL parameters used to label campaign traffic, including source, medium and campaign.
CPC
Cost per click; also the default paid-traffic medium label described in the Q&A.
API
An interface that lets software access data and capabilities programmatically.
MCP
Model Context Protocol: a way for AI applications to connect to external tools and data.
Workflow
A configured set of rules and actions that automates handling of qualifying accounts.
